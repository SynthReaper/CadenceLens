CadenceLens: Speaker-Agnostic Acoustic Normalization, Dynamic Time Warping, and Causal Prosody Diagnosis for Contrastive Oratory Assessment

KamandPrompt AI Consortium & DevSwarm Research Group • Indian Institute of Technology (IIT), Mandi • Track C: Contrastive Speech Analytics
Abstract

Automated evaluation of spoken rhetoric has historically suffered from high inter-judge subjectivity and biological bias. Traditional ASR benchmarks evaluate lexical transcription accuracy, but systematically ignore prosodic mastery, phrasing, and temporal cadence. We present CadenceLens, a deterministic speech forensic system combining speaker-agnostic acoustic normalization (semitone pitch centering and Burg LPC formant dispersion), non-linear Dynamic Time Warping (DTW), and a multi-tier causal reasoning engine. Accompanied by the Contrastive-Oratory-2026 benchmark—a curated corpus pairing 5 historical masterpieces with 4 synthetic defect tiers on identical textual substrates—we demonstrate monotonic degradation proof of the Composite Delivery Index ($CDI$) and zero-hallucination millisecond flaw localization.

Keywords: Acoustic Phonetics, Contrastive Speech Evaluation, Dynamic Time Warping, Fundamental Frequency Semitones, Praat Parselmouth, Causal Explanation, Vowel Space Formants.

§ 1. Problem Formulation & The Contrastive Corpus Paradigm

Assessing rhetorical eloquence has long been impeded by two structural bottlenecks: (1) Acoustic Confounding, wherein biological pitch variations between speakers (e.g. low-pitch baritones versus high-pitch tenors or sopranos) skew naive fundamental frequency ($F_0$) models; and (2) Lack of Counterfactual Data, wherein speech datasets compare completely disparate texts, making it impossible to disentangle whether delivery differences stem from lexical syntax or phonetic prosody.

To resolve this, we formalize the contrastive evaluation paradigm. Let $\mathcal{W} = \{w_1, w_2, \dots, w_K\}$ denote an invariant lexical transcript. We construct pair-wise acoustic realizations: a canonical master reference $\mathcal{A}_{\text{base}} = \Phi(\mathcal{W})$ executed with master prosodic phrasing, and an evaluated utterance $\mathcal{A}_{\text{test}} = \Phi'(\mathcal{W})$ exhibiting controlled prosodic flaws across four systematic tiers:

Tier 1: Master

Gold-standard cadence, pristine pauses, and expressive intonation.

Tier 2: Near-Perfect

Accurate delivery with minor timing or phrase boundary deviations.

Tier 3: Moderate Flaws

Unwarranted pauses within syntactic constituents and localized monotone.

Tier 4: Botched

Severe rushing/dragging, misplaced contrastive stress, and vocal instability.

§ 2. Speaker-Agnostic Acoustic Normalization

Direct linear comparison of pitch in Hertz ($Hz$) penalizes female speakers compared to male speakers due to vocal cord dimensions. We eliminate this biological anatomical bias by transforming all continuous voiced $F_0$ frames into semitones centered on each individual speaker's median fundamental frequency:

$$F_{0,\text{ST}}(t) = 12 \cdot \log_2 \left( \frac{F_0(t)}{\text{Median}(F_{0,\text{voiced}})} \right)$$

Under this logarithmic formulation, a rhetorical melodic rise of $+4.0\text{ ST}$ (a major third musical interval) produces an identical numerical trajectory whether performed by an adult male ($110\text{ Hz} \to 138.6\text{ Hz}$), an adult female ($220\text{ Hz} \to 277.2\text{ Hz}$), or a child. Dynamic range, standard deviation, and contours become entirely speaker-invariant.

§ 3. Praat Clinical Forensic Phonetic Extraction

We invoke the native Praat phonetic engine via C-bindings to calculate micro-acoustic perturbation features directly from the acoustic pressure wave:

Harmonics-to-Noise Ratio (HNR):

Measures the ratio of periodic acoustic glottal energy to turbulent aerodynamic aspiration noise in decibels:

$$\text{HNR} = 10 \log_{10} \left( \frac{r(0)}{1 - r(0)} \right) \text{ dB}$$

Healthy rhetorical projection exceeds $15.0\text{ dB}$; lower values indicate breathiness or strain.

Vocal Perturbations (Jitter & Shimmer):

Cycle-to-cycle frequency variations (Jitter) and peak amplitude variations (Shimmer) measure motor-control stability:

$$\text{Jitter} = \cfrac{\cfrac{1}{N-1} \sum_{i=1}^{N-1} |T_i - T_{i+1}|}{\cfrac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N T_i} \times 100\%$$

Clinical thresholds: Local Jitter $\le 1.04\%$, Local Shimmer $\le 3.81\%$.

Furthermore, we extract formant frequencies $F_1$ (vocal tract height / jaw aperture) and $F_2$ (tongue front-back position) using the Burg Linear Predictive Coding (LPC) algorithm. Under severe delivery defects (e.g. mumbling or breathless rushing), the speaker's vowel quadrilateral area shrinks toward the central schwa coordinate ($[500, 1500]\text{ Hz}$), providing objective anatomical evidence of sloppy articulation.

§ 4. Non-Linear Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) & Temporal Grounding

To compare temporal features across orations of unequal speaking duration, we construct an acoustic feature vector $\mathbf{f}(t) = [F_{0,\text{ST}}(t), E_{\text{RMS}}(t), \Delta F_{0,\text{ST}}(t)]^T$. We compute the optimal non-linear warping path $p = (p_1, \dots, p_L)$ minimizing cumulative Euclidean distance:

$$D(i, j) = \|\mathbf{f}_{\text{test}}(i) - \mathbf{f}_{\text{base}}(j)\|_2 + \min \left\{ D(i-1, j), D(i, j-1), D(i-1, j-1) \right\}$$

Once aligned, temporal flaw grounding is computed via dual-hysteresis thresholding on the warping path derivative $\dot{w}(t) = \frac{dt_{\text{base}}}{dt_{\text{test}}}$. Intervals where $\dot{w}(t) \ll 0.4$ define Dragged Cadence, while $\dot{w}(t) \gg 2.2$ identify Rushed Phrasing. Unwarranted silence intervals ($\tau_{\text{pause}} > 0.45\text{s}$) splitting uninterrupted master syntactic units trigger instantaneous Inappropriate Pause incident flags.

§ 5. The Composite Delivery Index (CDI) Formulation

The overall oratorical performance is synthesized into a single bounded, mathematically rigorous metric $\text{CDI} \in [0, 100]$ composed of four weighted dimensions:

$$\text{CDI} = 0.25 \cdot S_{\text{pacing}} + 0.30 \cdot S_{\text{pitch}} + 0.20 \cdot S_{\text{stress}} + 0.25 \cdot S_{\text{fluency}}$$

Each component sub-score is bounded in $[0, 100]$:

  • Pacing Score ($S_{\text{pacing}}$): Penalizes fractional tempo divergence $|\frac{\text{WPM}_{\text{test}} - \text{WPM}_{\text{base}}}{\text{WPM}_{\text{base}}}|$ and rate-swings.
  • Pitch Prosody ($S_{\text{pitch}}$): Computed via exponential decay of the normalized DTW pitch cost: $100 \cdot \exp(-\text{Cost}_{\text{pitch}} / 2.5) - 10 \cdot N_{\text{monotone}}$.
  • Stress Dynamics ($S_{\text{stress}}$): Evaluates Kullback-Leibler divergence between RMS energy distributions and peak prominence alignment.
  • Fluency Score ($S_{\text{fluency}}$): Accumulates penalty weights over total inappropriate pause duration $\sum \tau_{\text{pause}}$ and hesitation count.
